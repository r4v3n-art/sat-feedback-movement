We study query and movement complexity of local search in a hidden-formula SAT model. A hidden CNF on N variables of width at most 3 is promised to have a satisfying assignment in which every clause has an odd number of true literals. That odd-parity promise makes the problem affine (XOR-SAT), solvable by Gaussian elimination (Schaefer 1978); our bounds concern query and movement cost and say nothing about general SAT or P versus NP. By contrast, 1-in-3-SAT (exactly one true literal per clause) is NP-complete. An oracle returns one violated clause per round; a legal move flips up to B variables, including at least one from the reported clause, with no free restarts or hypothetical queries.

We obtain a tight minimax bound of Theta(N + N^2/B) moves for 1 <= B <= N. The upper bound is a deterministic polynomial-time solver that learns affine parity equations over F_2 and repairs a guide by sparse pivot elimination, using at most r(r+1)/2 flips when B=1 and at most sum_{i=1}^r ceil(i/B) moves in general, with r <= N the final learned rank. The lower bound uses linear-size, bounded-occurrence instances from constant-rate, linear-distance LDPC codes: every randomized solver needs Omega(N + N^2/B) expected moves under a fixed reporting order. Under identical uniform-clause feedback, the uniform random-walk policy (by Proposition 4.1, the only memoryless single-flip policy equivariant under signed renamings) needs Theta(2^N) expected flips on a dense family, and Omega((2-eps)^N) for every 0 < eps < 1 on a bounded-occurrence family with O_eps(N) clauses, while a symmetry-respecting parity learner keeps its O(N^2) pathwise guarantee. Lean 4 verifies the three movement and separation results, learner termination, and polynomial bit complexity in a dense bit-operation model.
